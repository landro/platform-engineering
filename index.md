# The Platform Engineering Manifesto

We are uncovering better ways of building and operating software delivery systems by doing it and helping others do it.
Through this work we have come to value:

---

**Developer outcomes** over operational complexity

**Platform as a product** over platform as a project

**Golden paths** over golden cages

**Self-service capability** over ticket-driven operations

---

That is, while there is value in the items on the right, we value the items on the left more.

---

## Principles

**1. Our highest priority is reducing developer cognitive load.**
Every platform decision should answer: does this help developers focus on delivering business value, or does it add friction they must carry?

**2. Treat the Internal Developer Platform as a living product.**
Platforms have no delivery date. They have users, roadmaps, feedback loops, and product lifecycles — and they must continuously evolve to remain useful.

**3. Developer adoption is the true measure of platform success.**
A platform nobody uses delivers no value, regardless of its technical sophistication. Usage, satisfaction, and time saved matter more than lines of infrastructure code.

**4. Make the right thing the easy thing — not the only thing.**
Golden paths should guide developers toward safe, reliable outcomes. They must always include escape hatches: teams who need to diverge responsibly should be supported, not punished.

**5. Everything as code.**
Infrastructure, pipelines, security policies, and golden path configurations belong in version control. They must be testable, reviewable, and repeatable — never tribal knowledge.

**6. Right-size your platform.**
Match complexity to organizational need. A simple pipeline platform is as valid as a fully-orchestrated IDP. Build for the problem you have today, not the hypothetical org you might become.

**7. Security and compliance are platform features, not gates.**
Security by design means guardrails are built into the golden path. Safety should be automatic for the developer who stays on path — invisible, not adversarial.

**8. Start with the backend, not the portal.**
The frontend makes the platform usable. The backend makes it useful. Build the foundation before building the interface.

**9. Measure from day one.**
Track adoption, lead time, developer sentiment, and time-to-environment from the start. Feedback loops and telemetry are not afterthoughts — they are how platforms improve.

**10. Platforms are people, process, and culture — amplified by tools.**
Tools are multipliers, but only when the right culture and clear processes already exist. No tool compensates for absent product thinking or absent stakeholder trust.

**11. Don't reinvent the wheel.**
Apply established architecture patterns and open standards before building bespoke solutions. Evaluate → Experiment → Embed → Evolve → Exit, and retire tools that no longer serve.

**12. Earn and maintain your mandate.**
The platform team is an internal startup. It must continuously communicate value, build stakeholder buy-in, and demonstrate impact — or the platform will be unused, deprecated, or made mandatory by force, all of which are failure modes.
